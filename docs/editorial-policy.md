# Editorial Policy

## Mission

Ploos Preparedness / Ploos Beredskap publishes practical preparedness material that helps ordinary people become more resilient without relying on fear, sensationalism or unsupported claims.

## Tone

Prefer:

- calm, concrete guidance;
- clear limitations and trade-offs;
- checklists, worksheets and exercises;
- realistic scenarios;
- low-cost and maintainable solutions;
- advice that remains useful offline.

Avoid:

- collapse fantasy presented as expectation;
- fear-based sales language;
- false certainty;
- prestige gear as a substitute for planning and practice;
- implying that ownership of equipment equals competence.

## Evidence and sourcing

Safety-, health-, legal-, electrical- and radio-regulatory claims should be sourced to appropriate authoritative material and carry a verification date where the information may change.

Sources should be prioritised roughly as follows:

1. competent public authority or regulator;
2. standards body or primary technical documentation;
3. manufacturer documentation for equipment-specific behaviour;
4. reputable specialist secondary source;
5. field observation, clearly labelled as such.

A source from one country does not establish the rule in another.

## Exercises

Books should favour practical exercises. Examples include:

- operate for a defined period without Internet access;
- locate information by broadcast radio;
- calculate household water storage;
- test lighting and battery runtime;
- prepare and verify a family communications plan;
- rotate a food reserve through normal meals.

Exercises must not require unlawful radio transmission, unsafe electrical work or other avoidable hazards.

## Product references

Named products may be used as worked examples, especially where hands-on testing is available, but content should distinguish universal principles from model-specific behaviour. Commercial relationships, if any, must be disclosed.
