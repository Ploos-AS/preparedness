# The First Minutes

The first minutes are about establishing what is happening without wasting resources or creating a second hazard. Use the priority **people → heat → information → water/food → comfort**.

## 0–5 minutes: make the situation safe

Get safe battery lighting. Check that everyone in the home is all right, especially children, older people and anyone dependent on powered medical or assistive equipment. Do not make candles the first choice, and do not start combustion equipment indoors merely because the power is out.

Look outside or check with a neighbour if that is easy to do: is only your home dark, or is the outage wider? Check breakers and residual-current protection only if you know how and can do so safely. Do not dismantle, rewire or improvise with the electrical installation.

Record the time the power failed. Keep the fridge and freezer closed.

## 5–30 minutes: establish information

Check the network operator's outage information and relevant official messages while mobile service and internet access are still available. Separate what you know from what you assume: a confirmed affected area, an estimated restoration time and weather conditions are more useful than rumours.

Put the phone into a power-saving mode. Avoid unnecessary video and other heavy data use. If the household has an agreed contact plan, send one concise status message rather than a stream of updates.

In a flat, also consider shared services: lifts, access systems, garage doors, water pressure and communal pumps may be affected even when the problem is not visible inside the flat.

## 30–60 minutes: move from event to plan

If power does not return quickly, activate the operating mode you prepared. In winter this means preserving heat early: close doors to unused rooms, put on warm clothing and establish a warm zone if appropriate. In summer, refrigeration, food and overheating may matter more.

Identify what actually needs electricity during the next few hours. Prioritise communications, necessary medical equipment and other critical loads before comfort. Do not connect an inverter, generator or reserve supply in a way that has not already been planned and tested safely.

Check water needs early if the home depends on a well pump, pressure pump or another powered system. Do not consume stored drinking water unnecessarily until you know whether the normal water supply is working.

## Before the first hour is over

You should now be able to answer four questions: Is everyone safe? Is the fault local or wider? Which critical functions may fail if the outage continues? Which plan will the household follow for the next few hours?

You may not have every answer yet. The aim is to proceed systematically and preserve options while you learn more about the situation.
