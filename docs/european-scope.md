# European Scope

## English edition

**Ploos Preparedness is written for a European audience.**

The default assumptions for English-language material are:

- Europe-first context rather than US-first context;
- SI units;
- 230 V / 50 Hz as the common electrical baseline unless a country differs;
- European product classes and infrastructure terminology where practical;
- British/International English spelling;
- explicit country scoping when rules, services or infrastructure vary.

The English edition must not silently import US-specific assumptions such as gallons, 120 V mains, NOAA Weather Radio, FCC rules, US emergency numbers or US-only radio services.

## Country differences

Europe is not one regulatory jurisdiction. Material must separate:

1. broadly European/common guidance;
2. EU/EEA-wide rules where genuinely applicable;
3. national rules and authorities;
4. optional examples from individual countries.

A claim about one country must not be generalised to Europe without evidence.

## Norway

The Norwegian edition, **Ploos Beredskap**, is Norway-first where national context matters. Norwegian authorities, emergency numbers, climate assumptions, infrastructure and regulations should be used in preference to generic European examples.

## Radio

Radio content requires especially careful country scoping. The ability of hardware to tune or transmit on a frequency does not establish that transmitting there is legal.

Books and tools must distinguish:

- receive-only operation;
- licence-exempt services;
- amateur radio privileges;
- repeaters and local coordination;
- emergency communications law;
- equipment-specific compliance requirements.

Country-specific radio material should include source and verification date.
