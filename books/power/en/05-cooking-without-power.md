# Cooking Without Power

Plan meals together with the cooking method. A cupboard full of dry food is less useful if every meal requires long cooking times, substantial fuel and extra water.

## Propane and other gas fuels

Propane is highly relevant to preparedness cooking. Camping cookers and grills may use propane, while other portable appliances use butane or fuel mixtures. The complete appliance, regulator, cylinder and intended use matter more than the fuel name alone.

Plan fuel consumption alongside the menu, store cylinders according to national rules, test the appliance in advance and establish a safe cooking location.

Portable gas burners and camping stoves using small canisters should be treated as **outdoor cooking equipment**. A fuel being propane does not make an outdoor appliance safe for indoor use.

## House, apartment and season

A house may provide a straightforward safe outdoor cooking location. Apartments can introduce balcony, common-area, building and fire-safety restrictions. Never move outdoor combustion equipment indoors simply because weather conditions are unpleasant.

Summer may make outdoor cooking easier, although wildfire or local fire restrictions can matter. Winter planning must keep the cooking method usable without compromising the home's heat-retention strategy.

Treat fuel as part of the menu: record which meals need heat, approximate cooking time, appliance and fuel, then test a complete preparedness meal.
