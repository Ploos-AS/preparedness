# Food, Water, Fridge and Freezer

A useful preparedness pantry contains food the household already eats and can rotate. During a power cut it must also match the available cooking equipment, stored water and fuel.

## Plan meals, not cans

Mark each planned meal as **0 — no cooking**, **1 — minimal cooking**, or **2 — normal preparedness cooking**. Keep enough level 0 and 1 meals that the household is not dependent on a working cooking location at every mealtime.

A seven-day plan can be built from a small set of repeatable breakfasts, cold lunches, low-cook hot meals and complete no-cook reserves. Dietary needs, allergies, children, medicines that interact with food, and pets require household-specific adaptation.

## Water and fuel belong to the menu

Cooking water comes from the same finite reserve as drinking water. Record the water and cooking time required by each meal.

Do the same for fuel. Record appliance, fuel, approximate consumption, cooking time and portions, then test representative meals. Wind, ambient temperature, cookware and lids can materially affect real consumption.

Propane can be a useful fuel for suitable equipment, but fuel type does not determine whether an appliance is safe indoors. Portable camping stoves and small gas burners are treated as outdoor cooking equipment unless explicitly approved otherwise.

## Refrigeration

Keep refrigerator and freezer doors closed as much as practical and decide in advance which foods would be used first. Food-safety decisions should be based on qualified time/temperature guidance rather than smell or taste alone.

Summer can make loss of refrigeration more urgent. Winter outdoor temperatures may sometimes help but an outdoor location is not automatically a controlled refrigerator: temperatures vary and food still needs hygienic protection and monitoring.

## Rotate

Treat the preparedness pantry as a buffer in the normal food system:

**buy → store → use → replace**

This reduces waste and reveals whether the planned food is something the household can actually live on.
