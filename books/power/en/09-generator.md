# Generator

A generator can provide substantial energy for as long as fuel remains available, but it also introduces exhaust, carbon monoxide, fire risk, noise, maintenance and electrical-integration issues. Treat it as a system that must be planned and tested.

Begin with the load list. Refrigeration, pumps or communications equipment may matter more than trying to power the entire home normally. Distinguish watts from watt-hours and account for starting loads.

Combustion generators belong outdoors, positioned according to the manufacturer's safety requirements so exhaust cannot enter occupied buildings.

Never improvise a connection that back-feeds household wiring. Connection to a building installation is a jurisdiction-specific electrical-safety matter requiring an approved design and appropriate professional competence.

Fuel storage, rotation, consumption and intended run schedule are part of the generator plan.
