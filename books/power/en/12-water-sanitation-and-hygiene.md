# Water, Sanitation and Hygiene

A power cut is not necessarily a water outage, but electricity and water systems are closely connected. Pumps, pressure boosting, wells, treatment and wastewater systems may all depend on power.

Water preparedness therefore begins with **stored drinking water**, not with an assumption that the tap will continue to work.

## 1. Where does the water come from?

Map the dwelling: public supply, private well, shared private supply, another source or a combination. Then identify which components between the source and the tap require electricity.

## 2. Public water supply

A public supply may continue during a power cut, but do not assume that it will do so indefinitely. Local topography, pressure zones, pumps, backup power and the incident itself can affect service.

Follow instructions from the local water authority, including requests to reduce consumption or boil water. Stored water remains useful even in a flat connected to a public supply.

## 3. House with a private well

An electric well pump makes electricity part of the water system.

Record its rated power, starting or peak demand, supply voltage, pressure vessel, how much water remains available before the pump must run again, whether the intended generator or inverter can actually start and operate it, and how the system can be tested safely.

A well pump can be one of the loads that determines the required peak capability of a generator system.

## 4. Pressure vessel

A pressurised system may provide some water after the electricity fails. The usable amount depends on the actual installation.

Do not assume that the vessel's nominal volume equals the amount available at the taps. Measure how much your system delivers from its normal state until the pump would otherwise start.

## 5. Hot-water cylinder

A hot-water cylinder can contain a substantial volume, but this book does **not** automatically treat that water as a drinking-water reserve.

Whether water should be drawn from a particular system depends on its design, water quality, hygiene, temperature and manufacturer instructions. Stored drinking water should therefore remain the primary drinking-water reserve.

The cylinder may nevertheless represent stored heat and water with practical value when the installation and safe use are properly understood.

## 6. When the water stops

Prioritise available clean water for drinking, necessary cooking, essential medical or personal needs, basic hygiene and then other purposes.

Distinguish **drinking water** from water suitable only for other tasks. Do not reduce necessary fluid intake merely to conserve supplies; plan an adequate reserve instead.

## 7. Toilets and wastewater

Having a bucket of water does not automatically mean a toilet should be flushed.

Establish whether the dwelling uses gravity drainage, a private septic system, sewage pump, macerator, lift station or another electrically dependent component. If wastewater cannot move onwards, additional flushing can make the situation worse.

Keep a basic sanitation fallback that does not assume normal drainage remains available.

## 8. Flat

Residents of a flat often control fewer parts of the water system. Pressure boosting and shared pumps may be building services.

Ask the building owner, housing association or equivalent how water and wastewater systems behave during a prolonged power cut and whether relevant shared equipment has backup power.

Stored water inside the flat is the part the household controls directly.

## 9. House

A house may offer more storage and alternative water options, but private pumps also place more responsibility on the owner.

Where a house has a well, generator and battery system, treat the water pump as its own qualified load in the energy plan.

## 10. Summer and winter

Hot weather can increase water demand and affect storage conditions.

In winter, frost can make the event more serious. Pipes, outdoor taps and parts of private water systems can freeze as the building cools. The winter plan therefore covers both **access to water** and **protection of the installation**.

## 11. Stored drinking water

Use the current preparedness guidance of the competent authority in your country for recommended quantity, storage, container hygiene and any treatment requirements.

The calculator can perform arithmetic; the authority determines the recommendation.

## 12. Test

A useful water exercise is simple: live for an agreed period from the stored reserve without disabling critical systems unsafely, measure actual consumption, prepare a planned meal, perform basic hygiene and check how much reserve remains.

That produces a household-specific water budget rather than merely a theoretical figure.
