# Summer vs Winter

The same power cut can become a very different event depending on season. A household should therefore maintain at least a **winter profile** and a **summer profile**.

## Winter

In winter, heat and heat loss can become critical early. The plan should cover:

- alternative heating and a warm zone
- warm clothing, duvets and sleeping bags
- ventilation when approved combustion-based heating is used
- frozen-pipe risk and possible effects on water supply
- longer darkness and greater lighting needs
- snow, ice and cold making travel, access and repairs more difficult
- reduced performance of some batteries and other energy-storage systems in cold conditions
- safe access to outdoor areas when cooking or generator operation must take place outside

If the dwelling cannot remain safely warm, identify an alternative place to stay before the event.

## Summer

Space heating is normally less important, but other problems can become more prominent:

- fridge and freezer management may become urgent sooner
- food may deteriorate faster at high ambient temperatures
- drinking-water demand may increase
- the dwelling may overheat without powered ventilation or cooling
- solar production may be substantially better than in winter but remains variable rather than guaranteed
- outdoor cooking may be easier, while wildfire risk and local fire restrictions can still apply

## Two tests

Where practical, run the power-cut drill in both a cold and a warm part of the year. A system that works very well on a summer evening may be inadequate on a dark winter night.
