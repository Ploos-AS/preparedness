# From 72 Hours to Seven Days

This scenario connects the systems in the book. It is not a prediction of how an outage will unfold; it is a decision exercise.

**0–15 minutes:** establish safe lighting, determine whether the problem is local, note the outage time, keep refrigeration closed, preserve phone power and check immediate human needs.

**15–60 minutes:** establish the situation: expected duration if known, weather, water, communications, heat and critical household needs.

**1–6 hours:** protect resources. Reduce unnecessary electrical loads, preserve refrigeration, establish the warm zone in winter and confirm water availability.

**6–12 hours:** if the outage appears prolonged, move into planned preparedness operation: cooking plan, battery/generator cycles where applicable, communications windows and resource logging.

**12–24 hours:** calculate endurance from actual consumption rather than the original estimate.

**Day 2:** establish a repeatable morning/day/evening routine.

**Day 3 / 72 hours:** explicitly choose normal preparedness operation, conservation, minimum operation or relocation.

**Days 4–5:** optimise for endurance and combine tasks where this saves scarce resources.

**Days 6–7:** retain a safety margin and consider the plan beyond day seven.

## House — winter

Heat, water and house-specific electrical loads can dominate. A hybrid system may divide functions between wood heat, batteries and a generator.

## Apartment — winter

Clothing/bedding, a warm zone, stored water, communications, low-energy loads and a realistic relocation option take priority. Do not assume generator or outdoor cooking options that the property cannot safely support.

## Summer

Refrigeration, food safety, drinking water and overheating may become more important, while solar may contribute without being guaranteed.

## Recovery

After power returns, replenish stores, recharge batteries, service equipment and write lessons back into the plan.
