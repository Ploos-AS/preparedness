# Generator

A generator can provide substantial energy for as long as fuel remains available. It also introduces exhaust, carbon monoxide, fire risk, noise, maintenance, fuel logistics and electrical-integration hazards. Treat it as a **system that must be planned and tested**, not merely a machine kept in storage.

## 1. Begin with the loads

Do not begin with “what size generator should I buy?”. Begin with **what actually has to work?**

For each critical load, record continuous watts, any starting or peak demand, expected run time, whether it can operate periodically and whether a battery could handle it instead.

Refrigeration, selected pumps, communications and battery charging may matter more than attempting to operate the whole home normally.

## 2. Continuous power and starting loads

Motors, compressors and some power supplies can demand substantially more power when starting than during steady operation. A generator that covers the sum of normal running loads may therefore still be inadequate.

Use measurements and documentation for your own equipment. This book does not treat generic starting-current multipliers as universal facts.

## 3. Do not run everything at once

A smaller generator can be useful when loads are scheduled. One operating cycle might run refrigeration and necessary pumps, charge the battery bank at the same time, operate another planned high-power load, then stop the generator and let the battery support lighting, phones, radio and other small loads.

Scheduling can reduce idle running, noise and fuel use.

## 4. Continuous versus periodic operation

Some loads may require continuous supply. Others tolerate pauses, particularly when a battery acts as a buffer.

Plan with fuel consumption per operating hour, operating hours per day, litres per day, planning days and a reserve. Start with manufacturer data and, where it can be done safely, measure real consumption at a representative load.

## 5. Generator plus battery

A hybrid arrangement can use both technologies:

**generator → high-power loads + battery charging → generator off → battery → small continuous loads**

This does not automatically make the system more energy-efficient. Charging losses, inverter losses, generator efficiency and the load profile still matter. The main advantage is flexibility and the possibility of reducing generator run time.

## 6. Fuel is stored energy

A generator without available fuel is not backup power.

Record the fuel type, permitted and safe storage arrangement, measured or documented consumption, rotation, the number of planned operating hours represented by the stock, a reserve, and how fuel might be obtained if electronic payments or local fuel pumps are unavailable.

Storage limits and safety requirements vary across Europe. Check current national and local rules rather than applying a universal quantity from another jurisdiction.

## 7. Placement

Operate combustion generators outdoors in accordance with the manufacturer's safety requirements. Position them so exhaust cannot enter occupied buildings through windows, doors, vents, garages or other openings.

“Inside the garage with the door open” is not a general-purpose safe solution.

The plan must also work in rain, snow, wind and darkness without moving the generator to an unsafe location.

## 8. Electrical connection

A standalone generator supplying suitable appliances and a system supplying a building installation are different engineering and regulatory problems.

**Never improvise a connection that back-feeds household wiring or the public network.**

A fixed or alternative supply to a building installation needs equipment designed for that purpose, safe isolation from the grid and work compliant with the rules and competence requirements of the relevant jurisdiction. This book does not provide instructions for modifying a 230 V installation.

## 9. Maintenance and test running

Include the manufacturer's maintenance intervals, oil and consumable checks, starting tests, realistic load tests, fuel rotation, date of the latest test and any defects requiring attention.

A generator that has never been started with the intended loads is an assumption, not a qualified preparedness system.

## 10. Example: house with wood heat, battery and generator

Use the wood stove for heat so that space heating does not dominate the electrical budget. Let the battery support small continuous needs such as lighting, phones and radio. Run the generator in planned periods for selected high-power loads and battery charging, while refrigeration follows a deliberate plan rather than automatically requiring the generator to run continuously.

This separates three functions:

- **wood → heat**
- **battery → quiet small loads**
- **generator + fuel → high power and recharging**

Failure of one part therefore need not remove every function.

## 11. Summer and winter

In winter, placement, starting, snow, wind, access and fuel handling all need to work in cold conditions. If the house normally uses electric heating, the required energy can become very large; an independent safe heat source can therefore change the generator calculation completely.

In summer, solar may contribute more to battery charging while refrigeration or a water pump can become higher priorities.

Include at least a winter test and a summer operating assessment.

## 12. Back-feeding can endanger other people

An incorrectly connected generator can energise wiring or equipment that other people reasonably believe to be isolated. Correct separation from the public network is therefore a safety requirement, not merely a household preference.

This book provides no male-to-male cable method, improvised inlet or other back-feeding procedure.
