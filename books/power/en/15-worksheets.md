# Worksheets

The worksheets turn the book from guidance into a concrete plan for your home. They are designed to be printable and usable without internet access, an account or Ploos software. Complete the minimum set first, then add detailed sheets for the systems you actually have.

## Start here: the minimum set

Begin with the **home profile**, **season profile**, **baseline inventory**, **power inventory**, **energy budget**, **water system**, **communications plan** and **resource endurance** sheets. Together they provide a first view of people, building, season, stores, critical loads, water, information and how long resources may last.

Then use the **outage timeline** during a real event or drill. It makes observations and decisions reviewable instead of relying on memory afterwards.

## Food, water and sanitation

The **seven-day menu** connects food stores to actual meals. **Cooking fuel** helps estimate the energy or fuel those meals require.

The **water system** sheet describes normal supply, electrical dependencies and reserves. Use the **water test log** when measuring actual capacity or consumption. The **sanitation plan** covers what the household will do if water, sewerage or other hygiene functions become limited.

The **cold-storage plan** records which food and appliances are prioritised. Use the **cold-storage log** to record time, temperature and actions during an event.

## Heat

The **heat system** sheet maps primary and backup heating, energy sources and the planned warm zone. The **heating fuel budget** makes stores and expected consumption visible. Use the **heat test log** during a drill or outage to compare the plan with actual temperature development.

## Battery, solar and backup power

The **battery system** sheet records capacity, power limits and relevant operating assumptions. The **solar plan** describes what solar generation can realistically contribute. **Operating modes** define which loads are used during normal, reduced and minimum operation.

The **generator plan**, **generator fuel budget** and **generator test log** belong together. The plan describes the system and safe operation, the budget describes endurance, and the log records what an actual test demonstrated.

Use the **hybrid energy plan** when several sources — for example battery, solar and generator — are intended to complement one another. Use **EV reserve power** only where the vehicle and the particular solution genuinely support the function being planned.

## Information and communications

The **communications plan** records channels, contacts and fallbacks. **Communications energy** makes the power needs of phones, radios, routers and other communications equipment visible. The **information log** separates time, source, information and decision, which is useful as a situation changes.

## Money and endurance

The **cash plan** helps the household think through payment alternatives without pretending that one fixed cash amount suits everyone. **Resource endurance** brings together how long water, food, energy, fuel and other limited resources are expected to last.

## Use the sheets as a system

Do not complete all 29 merely because they exist. Select worksheets for the home and equipment. A flat without a generator does not need a generator log; a home with a private well gains much more from the water sheets.

When one sheet changes, consider its dependencies. A new fridge affects the power inventory and energy budget. A new wood stove affects the heat system and fuel budget. An EV-based reserve-power arrangement may affect both the energy plan and the transport reserve.

## Paper first, software optional

Keep the operational plan and the most important worksheets on paper. Digital copies are useful for maintenance, but should not be the only copies.

The **ploos-prep** command-line tool can make selected calculations faster and repeatable. It is an aid, not a requirement: the numbers and decisions should remain understandable without the program.

After every power-cut drill, update the worksheets with what you actually measured. Preparedness then develops from assumptions into documented experience.
