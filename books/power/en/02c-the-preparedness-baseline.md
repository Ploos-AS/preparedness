# The Preparedness Baseline

A power cut is easier to manage when the household already maintains a basic preparedness reserve.

The common Ploos Preparedness model covers **water, food, heat and clothing, light and energy, information and communications, payments, hygiene, medicines/first aid and household-specific needs**.

Official recommended duration and quantities vary by country. The European edition therefore keeps the common planning method separate from national recommendations.

## Water

Keep a deliberate drinking-water reserve. Use the current guidance of the competent authority in your country to set the planning quantity and storage method, then adapt it for household-specific needs.

Remember that water used in food preparation comes from the same finite reserve.

## Food

The food reserve should not be a forgotten emergency box. Choose food the household actually eats, rotate it through normal use and make sure a meaningful portion can be eaten without electricity or with very little cooking energy.

## Payments and cash

A power cut can affect electronic payments, terminals, communications and cash machines.

Use more than one practical payment method where available. The exact mix of cards, cash and accounts depends on the country's payment infrastructure and the household's needs.

This book does **not** prescribe one universal cash amount. Estimate what necessary purchases might still have to be made during the planning period and keep the payment fallback proportionate to that need.

## The rest of the baseline

Warm clothing and bedding, lighting, batteries or battery storage, access to information, hygiene supplies, necessary medicines and first aid, and the needs of pets or other household members belong in the baseline as well.

The rest of this book shows how that general preparedness baseline changes when the specific event is a prolonged power cut.
