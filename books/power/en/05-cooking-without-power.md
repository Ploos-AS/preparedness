# Cooking Without Power

Plan meals together with the cooking method. A cupboard full of dry food is less useful if every meal requires long cooking times, substantial fuel and extra water.

## First level: food without cooking

Keep some meals that can be eaten cold or need only minimal heating. This saves fuel and simplifies the first hours of an outage.

## LPG, butane and other gas fuels

LPG is highly relevant to preparedness cooking. Camping cookers and grills may use propane or LPG cylinder systems, while other portable appliances use butane or fuel mixtures.

Do not choose a system from the fuel name alone. Consider the complete arrangement:

- appliance and regulator must suit the container and fuel
- estimate how many meals a realistic fuel quantity provides
- storage must comply with current rules
- test the appliance before it is needed
- provide a safe cooking location and ignition method
- keep a fallback if weather or other conditions make that location unusable

## Outdoor use matters

Portable camping stoves and small gas burners should be treated as **outdoor cooking equipment** unless the appliance is explicitly approved for another use under the applicable guidance.

A fuel being propane does not make an outdoor appliance safe for indoor use. An indoor-approved gas space heater is a different product category from an outdoor cooking appliance.

## House versus flat

A house may provide a straightforward safe outdoor cooking location. In a flat, balconies, common areas, building rules and fire safety can impose tighter constraints.

Do not move outdoor combustion equipment indoors merely because it is raining, windy or cold.

## Summer versus winter

Summer can make outdoor cooking easier, although wildfire danger and local fire restrictions may limit open flames.

In winter, the cooking location must remain usable without compromising the home's heat-retention plan—for example by leaving doors open—or moving combustion equipment into an enclosed space for comfort.

## Fuel is part of the menu

A seven-day food plan should identify which meals need heat, approximately how long they need heating, which appliance will be used and what fuel it consumes.

Test at least one complete preparedness meal using the equipment you actually intend to rely on.
